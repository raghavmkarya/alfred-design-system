Style lab notes

Adopting this

What it would take to bring aurora glass into the Alfred design system, and where it fights the brand.

What the design system would add

  • A glass surface token set: --glass-fill rgba(255,255,255,0.05), --glass-border rgba(255,255,255,0.12), --glass-blur blur(20px) saturate(160%), plus the lit-rim inset highlight for dark-theme nav and cards.
  • An aurora backdrop rebuilt in brand hues: periwinkle #A7A7FC as the core with an orange #FF8431 edge blob over ink #02021E, two or three blurred radial fields at 0.2 to 0.35 opacity. The aurora becomes that view's single gradient element, keeping the one-gradient-per-view rule.
  • Colored glow shadow tokens: --glow-orange 0 0 80px rgba(255,132,49,0.25), close to the existing primary-button hover glow, and a new --glow-periwinkle for glass panels.
  • A 2 to 3% noise overlay token to stop gradient banding on large dark surfaces.
  • A motion grammar: 20 to 40s drift on background gradients, fade-up reveals on cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), and a static fallback under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • No new fonts: Clash Display already covers the tight-tracked display role and Satoshi covers the Inter role, so we adopt only the tracking and scale discipline.

What clashes with the Alfred brand

  • The cool violet, cyan and magenta neon palette would dilute the orange and periwinkle two-accent brand. Aurora hues must stay strictly periwinkle, orange, and their blend.
  • Gradient-clipped headline text fights the one-gradient-per-view rule and the sacred mark gradient. Use it sparingly or skip it.
  • Aurora glass is dark-native, so it stays marketing-site-only: no layered glass in the light product app. And Alfred keeps Clash Display's characterful voice over the style's understated 500-weight everyman type.
