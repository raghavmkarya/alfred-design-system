Style lab notes

Adopting this

Enterprise trust is a proof machine: logo strip, stat band, badge wall in the first two scrolls. The structure travels well into Alfred's dark theme. The navy corporate skin does not.

What the design system would add

  • Proof-stack layout components: LogoStrip, StatBand (Clash Display numerals, Satoshi labels) and BadgeWall, so credibility lands in the first two scrolls of any marketing page.
  • Quantified-claim discipline as tokens: a --text-citation style (12 to 13px, muted) for fine-print sources on every stat, plus a count-up motion primitive (250ms ease-out, honours reduced motion).
  • A steel neutral ramp for dense proof surfaces: --steel-300 #B4C8E1, --steel-500 #5B6B7F and a --hairline rgba(167,167,252,0.15) border token for cards, tables and logo walls, keeping orange strictly for action.
  • Mega-footer and resource-card grid conventions as the marketing site scales; the dual-CTA hero (solid orange primary plus ghost secondary) is already brand-compatible.
  • Badge walls, partially: keep the pattern but re-house third-party badges on ink #02021E cards with periwinkle hairlines, isolated in one dedicated band, because analyst badge artwork is fixed-colour.

What clashes with the Alfred brand

  • The navy #032D60 palette itself: Alfred's canvas is ink #02021E, and a corporate navy would read as a second blue fighting periwinkle. Every navy role maps to ink instead.
  • Conservative type and dead interaction: one neutral grotesk at 600 to 700, 4 to 8px radii and no-glow hovers would erase Clash Display's character, Alfred's soft 12/24/32px radii and the warm orange hover glow. Borrow only the scale discipline and the hairline card borders.
  • Corporate stock photography and restraint as identity: Alfred stays with product UI, data visualisation and its hand-crafted illustration library, and keeps its one gradient element per view rather than adopting enterprise blandness.
