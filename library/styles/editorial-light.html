Alfred by E902 · seekalfred.ai

Cream, ink & one accent

Style lab notes

Adopting this

What the Alfred design system would take from Editorial light, and where it collides with the brand.

What the design system would add

  • A paper surface token set (--paper-50 #FAF9F5, --paper-100 #F0EEE6, --paper-border #E8E6DC, warm --ink-body #141413) as a marketing-light or docs and report theme, without touching the dark site.
  • A hairline card variant: 1px --paper-border, 12 to 16px radius, shadow removed, for reports, decision memos and long-form "Alfred writes to you" surfaces where the paper metaphor suits the chief-of-staff voice.
  • Whitespace and measure rules as tokens: 120px+ section padding, a 66ch prose cap, and the dated-list editorial index pattern. None of it conflicts with the brand.
  • A slow motion preset: 250ms ease-out rise-in with 60 to 80ms stagger, replacing scale and glow on editorial surfaces.
  • One editorial display decision, not two: either promote Bricolage Grotesque at weight 400 to the "one serif sentence" hero role, or add a true serif as --font-editorial. Adding both duplicates the accent role.
  • Illustration guidelines lifted from the style's restraint: single colour, line-led, no glossy 3D.

What clashes with the brand

  • Dark-first identity: the cream canvas is the style's core, but Alfred's marketing site is ink-dark by rule. Wholesale adoption is a rebrand; the workable translation is "editorial dark" (keep --ink-900, borrow hairline rules, large-display restraint and one-accent discipline).
  • Accent temperature: this style runs on desaturated terracotta, while Alfred's #FF8431 and the periwinkle-to-orange gradient are deliberately vivid signatures. Keep the vivid orange and adopt only the "accent on a leash" budget.
  • Type voice: Clash Display's tight-tracked geometric character is the opposite of a 400-weight serif. Never set the two in the same view, and never tighten or bold an adopted serif.
