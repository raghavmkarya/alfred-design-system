What changes
Your collections team always knows the right account to call.
Every morning, every account in your AR is ranked by Collection Priority Score: a formula that accounts for financial exposure, payment behaviour, broken promise history, and statutory deadlines. Your team starts the day knowing exactly where to go. No guessing. No gut calls. No time spent deciding.
No collection window closes without a flag.
I track payment drift at the account level using each customer's own historical behaviour, not their contractual terms. A customer who has always paid in 38 days and is now taking 52 is flagged weeks before their invoice goes overdue. Early is the only time that matters in collections.
Disputed invoices get a stance, not just a status.
Every deduction is classified into exactly one category: legally required and never chaseable, contractually agreed and close it out, or taken unilaterally and absolutely chaseable. The chaseable ones get a winnability score, an evidence pack, and a decay rate so you always know what is worth the fight.
Working capital stops sitting idle in your AR.
Unapplied cash, stalled disputes, and unresolved payment advices are working capital you already own but cannot deploy: into inventory, into headcount, into the next market. I identify unapplied payments, match them to the right invoices, and move them through application same day, not at month end.
Your collections operation scales without headcount scaling with it.
I rank every account so your team focuses where it changes an outcome. One lean team can manage a customer base many times its founding size when every hour goes to the account where the call makes a difference. The constraint on how fast you can add customers stops being your collections function.
What I do
Collections Worklist
Every account in your AR book, ranked daily by Collection Priority Score: exposure, days past due, broken promise history, urgency signals. Every item is derived from a real condition in the data and disappears the moment that condition clears. Nothing manually added, nothing manually removed. The list is always exactly what needs attention today.
Early Warning
I build a payment baseline for every customer using their own settled invoice history, not their stated terms. The signal is drift from their own normal, not deviation from the contract. A customer who has always taken 44 days on 45-day terms is not late and never was. The signal is when they start taking 58. Five bands: Improving, Stable, Slipping, Drifting, Deteriorating.
Cash Application
I turn identified payments into confirmed invoice settlements. I compose settling power from every source available: the payment itself, idle on-account cash, unapplied credit notes and state every exclusion explicitly so every allocation is auditable. Auto-post triggers only when the payment is advice-directed, reconciles to zero, and identification confidence is at or above 95%. Anything short goes to a human.
Disputes and Deductions
Every deduction is classified at the point of cash application: statutory, agreed, or claimed. Only claimed deductions are ever chased. For each one, I name the specific documents that would move winnability, who holds each document, and its exact point value. Winnability decays as evidence goes unfetched: the decay rate is visible before the claim is lost, not discovered afterward.
Promises to Pay
A promise to pay is not a flag on an invoice. It is an entity: carrying a date, an amount, a channel, an author, and a history. Lifecycle states are derived from payment activity, never set manually. A promise taken against an already-disputed invoice is flagged as not genuine. Two broken promises within 90 days is treated as a credit event. Your team knows which promises are real before they rely on them.
Concentration Risk
I measure six axes of AR concentration independently: counterparty, correlated, operational, key-person, temporal, and contested. The overdue gap: share of overdue minus share of AR: catches what concentration alone misses: a large positive gap is a problem customer, not a concentration risk, and the two need different responses. Compounds, where two risks stack on the same accounts, are deduplicated: ten warnings about one problem is nine warnings too many.
